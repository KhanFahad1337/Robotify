Backup Power Guide

Router Backup Hours, Explained

Published 26 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

"How many hours will it last?" is the first question everyone asks about a router power bank — and the mAh number on the box is the worst way to answer it. Two banks rated 10000mAh can give your router wildly different runtimes. Here's the one formula that actually predicts your hours, with worked examples you can copy.

The formula: hours ≈ usable Wh × 0.85 ÷ router watts — where usable Wh ≈ mAh × 3.7 ÷ 1000 for internal cells. Everything else is detail.

Step 1: find your router's watts

Flip the router (or its adapter) over and read the label — it shows volts and amps, like 12V ⎓ 1A. Multiply:

  • 12V × 0.5A = 6W — typical single-band home router
  • 12V × 1A = 12W — common dual-band / ONT-router combos
  • 9V × 0.6A ≈ 5.4W — many small routers

No label or faded print? See how to check your router's voltage — it takes two minutes and you should do it before buying anything anyway.

Step 2: convert mAh to watt-hours

Battery cells inside power banks are charged at 3.7V, but your router runs at 9V or 12V. mAh alone ignores that difference. Watt-hours (Wh) don't — they measure real energy. Convert with:

Wh = mAh × 3.7 ÷ 1000

Advertised capacityReal energyNotes
10000mAh≈ 37WhStandard router power bank size
20000mAh≈ 74WhLong cuts, or router + ONT together
12V / 5000mAh rating60WhSome brands rate at output voltage — read carefully

If a brand rates capacity at 12V, you can use it directly: 12V × 5Ah = 60Wh. Same math, different starting point. Confusing on purpose? A little — which is why Wh is the number to compare across brands.

Step 3: subtract conversion losses

Your router runs on 12V; the cells deliver 3.7V. A boost converter makes up the difference, and it wastes about 10–20% of the energy as heat. Counting on ~85% of rated energy reaching the router keeps your estimate honest instead of optimistic.

Worked examples

That last pair of rows is why combined load matters: if your ONT is a separate box, you're backing up two devices, not one — double the watts, half the hours. Our typical "4–6 hours" figure assumes a normal single router on a 10000mAh-class bank.

What eats hours in real life

  • Charging your phone too. Every watt a phone pulls is a watt not feeding the router — sharing one battery can cost you a third of your runtime.
  • Busy WiFi. Heavy downloads raise the router's draw slightly; idle browsing is the frugal case.
  • Battery age. After 2–3 years, expect 70–80% of original capacity. Buying one size up protects your estimate.
  • Heat. Karachi summers shave a little off both capacity and converter efficiency.

Getting more from every charge

  1. Back up only what matters — if the ONT is the ISP's wall-mounted box, ask us whether it's compatible before adding it to the load.
  2. Keep the phone off the bank during an outage; use mobile data if you must.
  3. Start with a full charge before the typical evening cut window.
  4. Sizing rule of thumb: 37Wh for router-only, 74Wh if you run router + ONT.

The bottom line

Ignore mAh at the shelf; convert to Wh, divide by your router's watts (after the ~85% haircut). A 10000mAh bank gives a typical router a full evening's cut — more than enough for most load-shedding blocks. Need the hardware? FAQ

Backup Hours, Answered

Why doesn't mAh tell me how long a router power bank lasts?

mAh measures charge at the battery's internal voltage (usually 3.7V), not energy usable by your router at 9V or 12V. To compare fairly, convert to watt-hours: Wh = mAh × 3.7 ÷ 1000. A 10000mAh bank stores about 37Wh, which is the number your router's runtime actually depends on.

How many hours does a power bank give a typical router?

A common 10000mAh (about 37Wh) router power bank runs a 6W router for roughly 4.5 to 5 hours after conversion losses, a 10W router for about 2.5 to 3 hours, and a router plus ONT combination (about 12–18W total) for roughly 1.5 to 2.5 hours.

Does charging my phone from the same power bank shorten router runtime?

Yes, roughly proportionally. Every watt the phone draws is a watt not going to the router. Charging a phone while powering the router can cut remaining runtime by a third or more, so treat them as sharing one battery.

Do backup hours drop as the battery gets older?

Yes. Rechargeable batteries lose capacity with age and charge cycles — after two to three years you may see 70–80% of the original runtime. Buying a slightly larger bank than you need gives you headroom for that natural decline.
